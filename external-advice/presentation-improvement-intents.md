# Presentation improvement intents — WN

Working brief · 16 September 2026

## Purpose and scope

Refine the existing OpenRNDR presentation into a coherent architectural experience through precise animation, clear content, and consistent visual detail. Preserve its concrete, component, blueprint, and catalogue character.

This document records what should improve and why. It is not an implementation plan. The original improvement inventory is retained below so no outstanding intent is lost. The agreed refinements in this first section take precedence wherever they qualify the original inventory. Questions and experiments remain open rather than becoming automatic implementation requirements.

The brief and working discussion are in English. The presentation itself uses natural Dutch, except established proper names such as **The Circle**.

## Agreed context and refinements

### Projection and fixed slide layout

The installation has two separate walls, each twelve metres wide, one on the left and one on the right. The audience is approximately eight to ten metres away.

During slides, the **left wall always carries the large title of the current chapter** and the **right wall carries the slide content**. Preserve this arrangement. Do not flag the persistent title as competing content simply because the desktop preview places both walls together.

Backdrops may take over both walls as one coordinated spatial experience. Assess their composition across the whole environment; each wall need not be independently complete.

Do not enlarge text merely because it appears small in the combined preview. Judge actual readability and hierarchy at projection scale. Empty space can be intentional and does not need filling by default. The original proposed shared caption size should therefore be treated as a coherent hierarchy to test, not a requirement that every type of caption use an identical size regardless of purpose.

### One family of building components

The agreed overall direction connects three visual treatments through the same component family:

- **Catalogue:** ordered collections, consistent viewpoints, spacing, numbering, and labels. Relevant to the opening, component collections, and sector walls.
- **Technical drawing:** reveal relationships from building to facade to individual component. Relevant to explanatory slides and case studies.
- **Sculptural backdrop:** give those same components physical presence through assembly, separation, lighting, and shadows across both walls.

Shared geometry, consistent colour roles, restrained concrete texture, and recognisable movement should hold these treatments together. Preserve variation within that system. A specific role for red, such as marking the current subject, remains a proposal to decide rather than an established rule.

### Floating red elements

The user found the floating red shapes awkwardly placed and chaotic. Retain the floating quality, but clarify the grouping, spacing, orientation, shadow relationships, and edge cropping. Give the composition a deliberate focus. The exact reported moment was not captured; an earlier image showed diagonal elements, an edge crop, and overlapping blue shadows, so the precise motion still needs review.

### Case studies as digital project files

**Confirmed decision:** use a sequence of composed views with precise transitions, rather than one continuous camera journey. Each view should have a deliberate framing and a clear subject.

The case studies should feel like moving through an organised digital project file, with neatly controlled animation and meaningful close-ups of facade details.

A proposed sequence is: introduce the project and drawing overview; move into a relevant facade or construction detail; highlight one element; hold with a short Dutch explanation; then return to the overview or continue to the next detail.

Keep project facts in stable, clear margins outside drawings and dimension lines. Use genuine project material. Missing facts remain visibly unresolved until supplied by WN.

### Dutch presentation copy

Apply Dutch consistently to headings, role descriptions, diagram labels, component metadata, captions, and calls to action. Preserve established names, particularly **The Circle**. Use natural construction terminology, consistent sentence case, correct compounds, and CO₂ throughout.

Additional details observed in sampled frames:

- Production labels mix sentence case and internal capitals.
- The lifecycle slide separates compounds such as grondstofwinning and fabricageplek.
- The database diagram uses inconsistent carbon notation.
- “Database van aangekocht materialen” has a grammatical error; “Database van aangekochte materialen” is a possible correction.
- Component metadata switches to English within Dutch content.
- An object identified as a beam accompanies a facade-element reduction claim.
- Repeated carbon-ladder headings remain over changing subjects.
- Chart axes need explicit measurement units and a clear explanation of the indexed measure. Do not infer incorrect values from a frame that may show an unfinished chart animation.

### Closing copy

The Circle is not a physical destination that can be visited. Replace the invitation to visit it.

**Proposed replacement: “Ontdek The Circle.”** Confirm the supporting action against the actual QR destination. Preserve the original aim of a strong action hierarchy and a possible return of the opening pen motif.

### Pacing, light, and sound: qualifications to test

The following discussion points qualify the baseline ideas but are not final design decisions:

- Stillness can support the speaker. Whole-wall light drift is an experiment, not a requirement for constant movement everywhere.
- Automatic playback should show complete scene content. In live use, consider graceful exit points rather than forcing the presenter to wait for every loop.
- Use a shared sound language across chapters. Equal cue density is not necessarily desirable; accents should follow meaningful events.

### Pinterest reference directions

Three screen captures of the WN board informed the visual discussion. Carry these principles into exploration:

- Simple volumes and strong cast shadows.
- Repeating facade grids and architectural modules.
- Ordered catalogue layouts and numbered component inventories.
- Folded planes carrying typography.
- Stacked or separated blocks and deliberate assembly.
- Lettering constructed from modular elements.
- Exploded construction drawings and extruded forms.

These are reference directions, not instructions to reproduce particular artworks. Animated pins were not played through, so their timing has not been assessed. The board remains the visual source; this file does not embed copies of the screen captures.

## Suggested order of work

Resolve content, terminology, and the closing action first. Then refine composition and case-study storytelling, followed by animation and transitions, and finally light, texture, and sound. Validate readability, contrast, and timing on the projection.

### Keeping attention through the evening

Each chapter needs one audience question, a recognisable situation, verified evidence, a visual reveal, a plain-language conclusion, and a bridge. The audience should be able to return after dinner without recalling an exact figure or following every animation.

Alternate explanation, evidence, and visual resolution according to the argument. Avoid a fixed attention-span rule or adding spectacle at arbitrary intervals. Let the large left chapter title remain the stable anchor during slides. The right wall carries the developing explanation; full-width backdrops provide spatial transitions and dinner atmosphere.

Write core and optional beats for each chapter. If service runs late, shorten optional supporting examples before sacrificing the evidence, takeaway, or bridge. Determine speaking durations with the host and catering team, not from the automated video runtime.

The two block-city scenes currently interrupt chapter four before its ESG synthesis. Retain the original intent to reduce or relocate them. Decide whether one earns its place by explaining the argument; otherwise move the visual material to an appropriate backdrop interval through a separate running-order decision. Do not silently change the saved order in this brief.

### Consistent course handover

1. Resolve the chapter's argument in one sentence.
2. Introduce the next chapter's actual question without withholding the current answer.
3. Release the room into the related course backdrop and a gentle sound bed.
4. Hold that atmosphere for the real service duration, with no essential information embedded in a loop.
5. Wait for the host or floor manager's readiness signal before resuming.
6. Reduce the dinner bed, run the next chapter opening once, and give a one-sentence recap.

Avoid important explanation during plate distribution or clearance. Define who gives the readiness signal during rehearsal. A delayed course should extend the atmosphere rather than force an automatic restart. Table prompts may be offered occasionally, but dinner should not become compulsory audience participation.

### Sound and visual continuity

Carry a recognisable visual family from each chapter into its course: catalogue to gallery, values to shadows, concrete to yard, The Circle and project discussion to a Circle-related dessert atmosphere. The final closing scene may return to the opening's component or drawing motif.

Use sound to distinguish talk, dinner, return, and exit. Give chapter openings identity without requiring equal numbers of accents. Allow background beds to give way to speech and meaningful sound marks. Build toward confidence and possibility, not simply increasing loudness or visual density.

### Decisions and rehearsal before implementation

Confirm the final audience action and destination, approved chapter takeaways, factual evidence, service windows, and the person controlling readiness. Prototype one full chapter ending, dinner hold, and next-chapter return. Rehearse a normal schedule, delayed service, a shortened speaking window, and a questions segment with little audience response.

Success means guests can explain the main point of each chapter, rejoin after a course, and understand the final action. The case studies feel like a guided project dossier, the dinner intervals feel intentional, and the closing is not lost after dessert.

## The evening as a whole

- **One visual system for the walls.** The slides are already one thing: black pane, white type, red
  for the point, one blue. The walls are three things — the house pair on black, relief in concrete
  grey, and two walls on white paper. Bring it down to two families: the catalogue walls in the house
  pair on black, the relief walls in concrete grey. The plain block city and the closing scene are the
  two to move.
- **One face.** The chapter titles are set in a grotesque and everything else in Rockwell, and the two
  now meet at the same size on every chapter. The titles should eventually be set from the chapter
  names in the deck's own face, so a renamed or added chapter needs no picture and the line breaks
  follow the rule by construction.
- **The concrete should be felt, not seen.** The grain sits well on the cards and the grey walls; on
  the saturated reds and blues of the belt and the plain wall it turns to mud. A lighter touch on the
  colour walls, the same texture everywhere.
- **Light that lives on the whole wall.** The chapter card's light now moves slowly. The same slow
  drift, far fainter, could sit in the projection over the whole frame, so nothing on the wall is ever
  quite still. To be judged on a filmed minute before deciding.
- **A wall is never cut mid-cycle.** A looping wall should be held for at least one full turn, in the
  export and in the auto cues, so what it was built to show is always shown whole.
- **A wall inside a chapter does not restart the chapter.** Stepping out of a backdrop back into the
  slides should not replay the card or the sting. Better still, walls live between chapters and in the
  courses, and the chapters hold only the talk.
- **Each course wall belongs to the chapter before it.** The gallery follows the catalogue chapter, the
  shadows the values, the yard the concrete — and the dessert wall after The Circle should carry The
  Circle's own elements rather than a placeholder chart.

## Chapter cards

- **The words stay whole.** Done: the smaller title pictures keep every word on its line.
- **The card is alive.** Done: the sun breathes and a lamp wanders across the wall, slowly, so the
  card is never a still while a chapter is talked over. The strength is one number, to be set on the
  projector.
- **Less bare wall at the start.** Every chapter opens on about three seconds of plain grid before the
  title reads. The reveal should stay, a little shorter.

## Walls

- **Who is speaking.** Half the wall is dark for the whole moment. Either the name fills its pane, or
  the other projector carries something — the mark, or a piece of the catalogue being drawn.
- **Programme.** The list is quiet and the right pane is a numeral. Two directions: show each chapter's
  own title picture beside its message so the programme previews the cards, or set the left as the
  evening's menu — four chapters large, the courses as rules between them — and let the right hold one
  message at a time.
- **The belt arrives on a cut**, not a dissolve out of the programme. A fade is for two states of one
  thing; this is a new subject.
- **Shadows wall.** The three forms take longer than the wall is held, so the last one is never seen.
  Hold it for a full round, and shorten the bare seam between forms.
- **Yard on black.** It reads as one red piece floating in black; the shadows do not show. Keep the
  black, tighten the row so three or four pieces stand in view, and give the shadow a navy that reads
  against the ground.
- **Block city.** Two versions back to back mid-chapter is fifty seconds of wall in the middle of the
  talk. Keep one, or move both out of the chapter.
- **De sectoren.** The sector is the subject and its name is set like a slide label. Set it at wall
  size, with the element and the counter under it. Fewer sectors, each with a real project, rather than
  twelve of which nine are guesses.
- **Case studies.** The facts lie across the drawings' dimension lines. Give the drawings a band and the
  facts the clear margins; the project's name larger, it is the one thing to read. The missing facts
  stay visible as missing until WN fills them.
- **Plain wall.** The ground swings red to navy every eight seconds through a dull purple. A wall
  changes at a wall's pace: slow it to a minute or more, or hold one colour and change it at the seam.
- **Ending.** The action line is the one thing the room is asked to do and it is the smallest thing on
  the wall. Action as the headline, the code at half the pane, the address in white. One idea to try:
  the code drawn by the opening wall's pen — a line travelling its modules and flooding — so the evening
  closes the way it opened.
- **The exit has sound.** The ending and the closing wall are silent now. A bed for the exit and a cue
  for the call to action.

## Slides

- **The city's last state holds too long on film.** The cull finishes early in a long click and the
  tree then opens on the same frame. Clicks should be able to have their own length within a slide, so
  a long push and a short cull are not the same click.
- **CO₂ behaald.** Nine bullets on a chart slide is a page, not a slide. Three a chart at body size, the
  rest in the speaker's notes.
- **CO₂ impact van beton.** The paragraph stands inside the red band and the column takes most of the
  pane. Narrow the column and set the paragraph beside it, or move it to the notes.
- **Demontabel in de stad.** The pieces on the roofs are small and at the edge; only their outlines
  read. Bring the camera in, or choose roofs nearer the middle.
- **The Circle in elementen.** The ring is grey where the guide's neutral is white. Decide when the mark
  arrives.
- **Three slides share the title "ESG Environmental — CO₂-prestatieladder."** On the domino it names a
  ladder over a decision tree. Ask WN whether the domino gets its own title or none.

## Content and wording

- **One sentence, one version.** The chapter 2 message is worded differently on the programme and on
  the quote. Pick one, and check "systemische" against "systematische".
- **One spelling of the fourth chapter's title** across the code, the programme and the picture.
- **Dutch, consistently.** Compounds closed (grondstofwinning, fabricageplek, energieproductie,
  levenscyclusanalyse), sentence case through the whole stack, CO₂ set as CO₂ everywhere, België with
  its diaeresis, and the two phrases nobody can read as written ("nul-loos processen", "C4 Ontdoen
  van") replaced with WN's words.
- **One vocabulary for the production chain.** Chapter 3 and chapter 4 describe the same five steps in
  two sets of words. Use one, in Dutch that a builder would say.
- **Pieces that match their claims.** On the hidden story a beam carries the facade figure and a wall
  the floor figure. Put the wall panel under gevelelementen, a floor piece under gewelven, a beam under
  gewapend beton.
- **Six countries named, not five.** Luxemburg appears twice; the sixth is Frankrijk.
- **Factories for this audience.** The list is Belgian and French with no Dutch site, under a speaker
  from Willy Naessens Nederland. Add the Netherlands, or say plainly it is the group.
- **Say which company the figures belong to.** 210 miljoen and 1,4 miljard are both true of something.
- **Let the certificate's exit say its line.** The slide is called "Geen compensatie" and the
  certificate leaves without a word. One line as it goes: niet compenseren, reduceren.
- **Callouts of two or three words**, not sentences, on the building.
- **Case-study words** a builder would use: magazijn or distributiecentrum rather than warenhuis,
  laaddocks rather than vrachtwagen bays; and the spelling of Kievits II checked.
- **The ending's line.** It repeats the chapter 4 quote. Fine as a callback; better if it is the one
  sentence the evening was building to, with a real action under it.

## Sound

- **Marks in every chapter.** Chapter 1 has a cue on every slide; chapters 2 to 4 have only the sting
  and the base loop. Give the other three the same density: an arrival mark per kind of slide, click
  marks where things are counted.
- **Four chapters, four stings.** One file opens every chapter now. A sting per chapter, or one voiced
  differently each time, tells the chapters apart by ear.
- **Beds that hand over.** The dinner beds cut at every wall boundary. A bed should fade out over the
  card's reveal and the next fade in under the wall, so the courses join the chapters rather than butt
  against them.
- **The loop ducks under the marks.** The base loop under the slides sits at one level; it should give
  way a little whenever a mark plays.
- **The arc the meeting asked for.** Mysterious at the arrival, uplifting by the exit. Choose and order
  the beds to that arc, and let the exit have its own.

## Type and layout

- **One size for a caption on a wall.** The walls set their captions at slide sizes while a wall carries
  one thing read across a room. One shared size for the sectors, the case studies, the ending's action
  and the speaker's name.
- **Text never lies on a drawing it does not belong to.** The blueprints and the CO₂ column both break
  this today.
- **Paragraphs keep one measure.** The ladder's paragraph is the model; every other paragraph should
  wrap to the same width and stand in clear space.

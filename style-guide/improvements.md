# Improvements to make

What should change in the show and why, after watching the filmed run of 15 September against the
style guide. Intent only — the findings with times and measurements are in
[review-2026-09-15.md](review-2026-09-15.md), and the how belongs in the drawer when each is taken up.

Two things are already done and listed at the end for completeness.

## The evening as a whole

- **One visual system for the walls.** The slides are already one thing: black pane, white type, red
  for the point, one blue. The walls are three things — the house pair on black, relief in concrete
  grey, and two walls on white paper. Bring it down to two families: the catalogue walls in the house
  pair on black, the relief walls in concrete grey. The plain block city and the closing scene are the
  two to move.
- **One face.** The chapter titles are set in a grotesque and everything else in Rockwell, and the two
  now meet at the same size on every chapter. The titles should eventually be set from the chapter
  names in the deck's own face, so a renamed or added chapter needs no picture and the line breaks
  follow the rule by construction.
- **The concrete should be felt, not seen.** The grain sits well on the cards and the grey walls; on
  the saturated reds and blues of the belt and the plain wall it turns to mud. A lighter touch on the
  colour walls, the same texture everywhere.
- **Light that lives on the whole wall.** The chapter card's light now moves slowly. The same slow
  drift, far fainter, could sit in the projection over the whole frame, so nothing on the wall is ever
  quite still. To be judged on a filmed minute before deciding.
- **A wall is never cut mid-cycle.** A looping wall should be held for at least one full turn, in the
  export and in the auto cues, so what it was built to show is always shown whole.
- **A wall inside a chapter does not restart the chapter.** Stepping out of a backdrop back into the
  slides should not replay the card or the sting. Better still, walls live between chapters and in the
  courses, and the chapters hold only the talk.
- **Each course wall belongs to the chapter before it.** The gallery follows the catalogue chapter, the
  shadows the values, the yard the concrete — and the dessert wall after The Circle should carry The
  Circle's own elements rather than a placeholder chart.

## Chapter cards

- **The words stay whole.** Done: the smaller title pictures keep every word on its line.
- **The card is alive.** Done: the sun breathes and a lamp wanders across the wall, slowly, so the
  card is never a still while a chapter is talked over. The strength is one number, to be set on the
  projector.
- **Less bare wall at the start.** Every chapter opens on about three seconds of plain grid before the
  title reads. The reveal should stay, a little shorter.

## Walls

- **Who is speaking.** Half the wall is dark for the whole moment. Either the name fills its pane, or
  the other projector carries something — the mark, or a piece of the catalogue being drawn.
- **Programme.** The list is quiet and the right pane is a numeral. Two directions: show each chapter's
  own title picture beside its message so the programme previews the cards, or set the left as the
  evening's menu — four chapters large, the courses as rules between them — and let the right hold one
  message at a time.
- **The belt arrives on a cut**, not a dissolve out of the programme. A fade is for two states of one
  thing; this is a new subject.
- **Shadows wall.** The three forms take longer than the wall is held, so the last one is never seen.
  Hold it for a full round, and shorten the bare seam between forms.
- **Yard on black.** It reads as one red piece floating in black; the shadows do not show. Keep the
  black, tighten the row so three or four pieces stand in view, and give the shadow a navy that reads
  against the ground.
- **Block city.** Two versions back to back mid-chapter is fifty seconds of wall in the middle of the
  talk. Keep one, or move both out of the chapter.
- **De sectoren.** The sector is the subject and its name is set like a slide label. Set it at wall
  size, with the element and the counter under it. Fewer sectors, each with a real project, rather than
  twelve of which nine are guesses.
- **Case studies.** The facts lie across the drawings' dimension lines. Give the drawings a band and the
  facts the clear margins; the project's name larger, it is the one thing to read. The missing facts
  stay visible as missing until WN fills them.
- **Plain wall.** The ground swings red to navy every eight seconds through a dull purple. A wall
  changes at a wall's pace: slow it to a minute or more, or hold one colour and change it at the seam.
- **Ending.** The action line is the one thing the room is asked to do and it is the smallest thing on
  the wall. Action as the headline, the code at half the pane, the address in white. One idea to try:
  the code drawn by the opening wall's pen — a line travelling its modules and flooding — so the evening
  closes the way it opened.
- **The exit has sound.** The ending and the closing wall are silent now. A bed for the exit and a cue
  for the call to action.

## Slides

- **The city's last state holds too long on film.** The cull finishes early in a long click and the
  tree then opens on the same frame. Clicks should be able to have their own length within a slide, so
  a long push and a short cull are not the same click.
- **CO₂ behaald.** Nine bullets on a chart slide is a page, not a slide. Three a chart at body size, the
  rest in the speaker's notes.
- **CO₂ impact van beton.** The paragraph stands inside the red band and the column takes most of the
  pane. Narrow the column and set the paragraph beside it, or move it to the notes.
- **Demontabel in de stad.** The pieces on the roofs are small and at the edge; only their outlines
  read. Bring the camera in, or choose roofs nearer the middle.
- **The Circle in elementen.** The ring is grey where the guide's neutral is white. Decide when the mark
  arrives.
- **Three slides share the title "ESG Environmental — CO₂-prestatieladder."** On the domino it names a
  ladder over a decision tree. Ask WN whether the domino gets its own title or none.

## Content and wording

- **One sentence, one version.** The chapter 2 message is worded differently on the programme and on
  the quote. Pick one, and check "systemische" against "systematische".
- **One spelling of the fourth chapter's title** across the code, the programme and the picture.
- **Dutch, consistently.** Compounds closed (grondstofwinning, fabricageplek, energieproductie,
  levenscyclusanalyse), sentence case through the whole stack, CO₂ set as CO₂ everywhere, België with
  its diaeresis, and the two phrases nobody can read as written ("nul-loos processen", "C4 Ontdoen
  van") replaced with WN's words.
- **One vocabulary for the production chain.** Chapter 3 and chapter 4 describe the same five steps in
  two sets of words. Use one, in Dutch that a builder would say.
- **Pieces that match their claims.** On the hidden story a beam carries the facade figure and a wall
  the floor figure. Put the wall panel under gevelelementen, a floor piece under gewelven, a beam under
  gewapend beton.
- **Six countries named, not five.** Luxemburg appears twice; the sixth is Frankrijk.
- **Factories for this audience.** The list is Belgian and French with no Dutch site, under a speaker
  from Willy Naessens Nederland. Add the Netherlands, or say plainly it is the group.
- **Say which company the figures belong to.** 210 miljoen and 1,4 miljard are both true of something.
- **Let the certificate's exit say its line.** The slide is called "Geen compensatie" and the
  certificate leaves without a word. One line as it goes: niet compenseren, reduceren.
- **Callouts of two or three words**, not sentences, on the building.
- **Case-study words** a builder would use: magazijn or distributiecentrum rather than warenhuis,
  laaddocks rather than vrachtwagen bays; and the spelling of Kievits II checked.
- **The ending's line.** It repeats the chapter 4 quote. Fine as a callback; better if it is the one
  sentence the evening was building to, with a real action under it.

## Sound

- **Marks in every chapter.** Chapter 1 has a cue on every slide; chapters 2 to 4 have only the sting
  and the base loop. Give the other three the same density: an arrival mark per kind of slide, click
  marks where things are counted.
- **Four chapters, four stings.** One file opens every chapter now. A sting per chapter, or one voiced
  differently each time, tells the chapters apart by ear.
- **Beds that hand over.** The dinner beds cut at every wall boundary. A bed should fade out over the
  card's reveal and the next fade in under the wall, so the courses join the chapters rather than butt
  against them.
- **The loop ducks under the marks.** The base loop under the slides sits at one level; it should give
  way a little whenever a mark plays.
- **The arc the meeting asked for.** Mysterious at the arrival, uplifting by the exit. Choose and order
  the beds to that arc, and let the exit have its own.

## Type and layout

- **One size for a caption on a wall.** The walls set their captions at slide sizes while a wall carries
  one thing read across a room. One shared size for the sectors, the case studies, the ending's action
  and the speaker's name.
- **Text never lies on a drawing it does not belong to.** The blueprints and the CO₂ column both break
  this today.
- **Paragraphs keep one measure.** The ladder's paragraph is the model; every other paragraph should
  wrap to the same width and stand in clear space.
